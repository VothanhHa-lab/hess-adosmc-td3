Table~\ref{tab:test} and Fig.~\ref{fig:testbars} summarize the five test items. All rows share tier~1 (A-DO-SMC), so the bus voltage differs only where a supervisor causes a power deficit.

\paragraph{Comparison with fixed parameters}
The filtered agent lowers the RMS battery current on every test cycle: by \R{us06a:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% on US06, \R{wh:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% on WLTC high, \R{wxa:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% on WLTC extra-high, and \R{wxb:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% on WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$ (paired over nine seed pairs; on WLTC high the paired changes range from \R{wh:v2vsafix:dibrmsmin} to \R{wh:v2vsafix:dibrmsmax}\,\%). The RMS rate of change of the battery current rises only slightly (\R{wh:v2vsafix:dslew}\,\% on WLTC high). No filtered agent saturates the command on the rated-load cycles, and the bus deviation stays at the tier-1 level (\R{us06a:v2:maxdev:mean}\,V on US06).

\paragraph{Comparison with the SOC rule}
The learned supervisor is not clearly better than the non-learning rule. It is \R{wxa:v2vsarule:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower than the rule on WLTC extra-high and \R{wh:v2vsarule:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower on WLTC high, but \R{us06a:v2vsarule:dibrms}\,\% higher on US06, where it wins \R{us06a:v2vsarule:wins} seed pairs on the training objective. The rule, which lengthens $\tau_b$ when the SC is well charged, already captures most of the benefit available on this plant.

\paragraph{Comparison with the MPC-EMS}
The MPC-EMS optimizes the same objective over the same actions behind the same filter. With the causal persistence forecast its RMS battery current is lower than the agent's mean on every test cycle; the agent's value is \R{us06a:v2vsmpcp:dibrms}\,\% higher on US06, \R{wh:v2vsmpcp:dibrms}\,\% on WLTC high and \R{wxa:v2vsmpcp:dibrms}\,\% on WLTC extra-high. The MPC-EMS obtains this by drawing the SC deeper: its minimum SC voltage is \R{us06a:mpcp:vmin}\,V on US06 and \R{wxb:mpcp:vmin}\,V on WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$, inside the penalized band below \SI{237.5}{V}. Measured by the full training objective, which penalizes that band, the agent wins \R{wxa:v2vsmpcp:wins} seed pairs on WLTC extra-high and \R{us06a:v2vsmpcp:wins} on US06, but \R{wh:v2vsmpcp:wins} on WLTC high. Neither supervisor dominates: the model-based one is better on battery current, the learned one keeps more SC energy on the longer high-power cycles.

\paragraph{Supervisory behavior}
The fixed and rule supervisors hold $v_{sc,ref}=\SI{312.5}{V}$. The agents lower the SC energy target instead (mean $v_{sc,ref}$ between \MVrefMeanVtwoMin\ and \MVrefMeanVtwoMax\,V across runs), and on US06 they keep it at the filter's lower bound (\MVloVtwo\,V) for \MAtBoundMin--\MAtBoundMax\,\% of the time (Fig.~\ref{fig:us06}). They also lengthen or switch $\tau_b$, and the agent shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:us06} uses observer gains well above the nominal value. Unlike on the reduced-storage plant (Section~\ref{sec:res_sizing}), the agents do not drive the recovery term to its limit: $|P_{rec}|$ never reaches $\PrecMax$\,kW in the logged runs. The MPC-EMS also lowers $v_{sc,ref}$ (mean \MPCVrefMeanMin--\MPCVrefMeanMax\,V) but varies it more strongly within a run (standard deviation up to \MPCVrefStdMax\,V).

\paragraph{Operation beyond the rated load}
With the US06 load scaled by 1.1, the fixed, rule and persistence MPC-EMS supervisors still do not saturate. One of the three filtered agents (seed 33) does, for \R{us06b:v2:sat:max}\,\% of the time, with a bus excursion of \R{us06b:v2:maxdev:max}\,V; preview MPC-EMS shows a similar event (\R{us06b:mpcv:sat}\,\%, \R{us06b:mpcv:maxdev}\,V). On WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$ no filtered supervisor saturates.

\subsection{Ablation of the Safety Filter}\label{sec:res_filter}
Without the filter, the agents push the SC to its lower voltage limit and cause power deficits even at rated load. On US06 the unfiltered agents saturate the command \R{us06a:nof:sat:mean}\,\% of the time on average, with bus excursions of up to \R{us06a:nof:maxdev:max}\,V; on US06$\times1.1$ and WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$ the excursions reach \R{us06b:nof:maxdev:max}\,V and \R{wxb:nof:maxdev:max}\,V. No run left the simulated admissible range on this plant. With the filter, the bus deviation on the rated-load cycles stays at the tier-1 level. On this plant filter v1 and v2 coincide, so the filter-version ablation is informative only on the reduced-storage plant (Section~\ref{sec:res_sizing}). The filter modified the $v_{sc,ref}$ component of the action in \FiltVscMin--\FiltVscMax\,\% of the decisions on the main test cycles, depending on the seed.

\subsection{Resistive Losses and Sensitivity to Vehicle Mass}\label{sec:res_loss}
\input{tab_loss.tex}
The RMS battery current is a proxy for battery stress, not for efficiency. Table~\ref{tab:loss} therefore reports the resistive losses in the battery and SC branches. Moving current from the battery to the SC lowers the battery loss but raises the SC loss, because the bank resistance ($R_{esr}+r_{sc}=\SI{59}{m\ohm}$) is of the same order as that of the battery branch. On average over the three seeds, the agent lowers the total resistive loss relative to fixed parameters on every cycle in Table~\ref{tab:loss} (by \LossTDMin\ to \LossTDMax\,\%), but one seed raises it by \LossTDUSSeedMax\,\% on US06; the SOC rule changes it by a few per cent in either direction, and the MPC-EMS lowers it most (by \LossMPCMin\ to \LossMPCMax\,\%). Switching, magnetic and drive-unit losses are not modelled, so these values do not give the drivetrain efficiency.

The vehicle mass of the main study (\SI{1830}{kg}) does not include the SC bank. Adding the mass of the three modules (\MassAddKg\,kg~\cite{maxwell125v}) and re-evaluating the fixed, rule, MPC-EMS and filter-v2 supervisors on US06 and WLTC high, without retraining, raised the RMS battery current of every supervisor by \MassIbIncMin--\MassIbIncMax\,\% and caused no saturation. The relative change of the agent with respect to fixed parameters was \MassDvtwoFixUS\,\% on US06 and \MassDvtwoFixWH\,\% on WLTC high, against \MassDvtwoFixRefUS\,\% and \MassDvtwoFixRefWH\,\% without the added mass (noise seed 101), so the comparisons above are not sensitive to this omission. Converter mass was not estimated.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Storage Sizing}\label{sec:res_sizing}
\input{tab_sizing.tex}
The same study was first carried out on a reduced-storage plant (Table~\ref{tab:params}): a lighter vehicle with a \SI{60}{kW} traction rating, a \SI{24}{kW} battery converter limit and a \SI{120}{F} SC, evaluated on the same drive cycles with the same training, selection and test protocol. Table~\ref{tab:sizing} compares the two plants.
\begin{itemize}
\item On the reduced-storage plant, the filtered agent caused power deficits on US06 (\G{g:us06a:v2sat}\,\% saturated time against \G{g:us06a:fixsat}\,\% for fixed parameters) and raised the RMS battery current on the long-haul segment by \G{g:lh:dv2fix}\,\%. There the agents switched $v_{sc,ref}$ rapidly and drove the recovery term to its $\pm\SI{5}{kW}$ limit, so that the recovery term acted as a bang-bang battery-power actuator. Unfiltered agents collapsed the bus in \G{g:us06a:noffails} runs on US06, and preview MPC-EMS collapsed it in \G{g:wxb:mpcvfails} of 3 runs on WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$.
\item On the Model 3 plant, with a battery limit of \SI{57}{kW} and a \SI{21}{F} SC, none of these failures appears on the rated-load cycles, and the agent lowers the battery current on every cycle.
\item On both plants the MPC-EMS achieves a lower RMS battery current than the agent (Table~\ref{tab:sizing}: \G{g:wh:dv2mpcp}\,\% and \G{m:wh:dv2mpcp}\,\% on WLTC high for the agent relative to MPC-EMS).
\end{itemize}
The failure modes of the learned supervisor therefore depend on how tightly the storage is sized relative to the load. The ranking against the model-based baseline does not.

\subsection{Computational Cost of the Supervisory Tier}\label{sec:res_cost}
On the simulation host, one tier-1 update (compiled) costs \TimeADOSMC\,\si{\micro s}. One tier-2 decision (actor with \NActorParams\ parameters plus safety filter, interpreted Python) costs \TimeAgent\,\si{\micro s} every \SI{100}{ms}. One MPC-EMS decision (\NCandMPC\ candidates, \HorMPCP\,s horizon, vectorized NumPy) has a median cost of \TimeMPCEMS\,ms, more than an order of magnitude above the actor but still only about 1\,\% of the decision period. Both figures are relative indicators only; execution on an automotive microcontroller was not measured.
