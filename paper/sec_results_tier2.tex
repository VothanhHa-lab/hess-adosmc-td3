\paragraph{Cycles inside the training power envelope}
On WLTC high and WLTC extra-high, the proposed supervisor (TD3 + filter v2) lowers the RMS battery current in all nine seed pairs, with no saturation:
\begin{itemize}
\item WLTC high: from \R{wh:afix:ibrms}\,A (fixed) and \R{wh:arule:ibrms}\,A (SOC rule) to \R{wh:v2:ibrms:mean}\,A, i.e.\ \R{wh:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower than fixed and \R{wh:v2vsarule:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower than the rule;
\item WLTC extra-high: \R{wxa:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower than fixed.
\end{itemize}
The bus voltage is unaffected: \R{wh:v2:maxdev:mean}\,V versus \R{wh:afix:maxdev}\,V on WLTC high. The price is a higher RMS rate of change of the battery current: \R{wxa:v2vsafix:dslew}\,\% versus fixed on WLTC extra-high.

\paragraph{How the agent achieves this}
The raw logs show that the agents do not use $v_{sc,ref}$ as a slowly varying reserve set-point, which is what it was intended to be. Instead they move it quickly: its standard deviation within a run is \VrefStdMin--\VrefStdMax\,V. As a result, the recovery term \eqref{eq:prec} sits at its $\pm\PrecMax$\,kW limit in \CapFracMin--\CapFracMax\,\% of the samples. With the fixed supervisor, the mean $|P_{rec}|$ is only \PrecWHfixedAbs\,kW on WLTC high. In effect the agent has turned the bounded recovery term into a bang-bang actuator for battery power. On transient cycles it switches this power against the load and so lowers the RMS battery current. The agent also keeps $\tau_b$ near its lower bound for most of the time (Fig.~\ref{fig:us06}), which explains the faster battery-current changes.

\paragraph{US06: out of the training envelope}
On US06 the same behaviour is harmful. The SC is depleted by long acceleration phases and needs sustained recharging. Here the switching policy delivers a mean recovery power of only \PrecUSvtwoMin--\PrecUSvtwoMax\,kW (seeds 11, 22, 33), against \PrecUSfixed\,kW for the fixed supervisor, which simply recharges at its limit. The SC voltage therefore falls to \R{us06a:v2:vmin:mean}\,V (fixed: \R{us06a:afix:vmin}\,V), below the power-capability level \eqref{eq:vpow}. The command is saturated \R{us06a:v2:sat:mean}\,\% of the time, whereas the fixed supervisor never saturates, and the bus-voltage excursion reaches \R{us06a:v2:maxdev:mean}\,V. This is not a loss of tier-1 stability, since the loop recovers once the demand falls, but it is an operating failure. The \R{us06a:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% lower RMS battery current does not compensate for it.

The reserve bound \eqref{eq:vlo} constrains the \emph{reference} $v_{sc,ref}$, not the SC voltage itself, and it is sized with the largest deficit of the training cycles ($E_{res}=\EresKJ$\,kJ). Neither property protects against a policy that keeps the reference high on average while withholding recharge power.

\paragraph{Loads beyond the rating}
With the load scaled by 1.1 (US06) or 1.2 (WLTC extra-high), the peak demand exceeds the vehicle rating used in \eqref{eq:vpow}. On US06$\times1.1$, every configuration saturates: fixed \R{us06b:afix:sat}\,\%, SOC rule \R{us06b:arule:sat}\,\%, v2 \R{us06b:v2:sat:mean}\,\%. On WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$, v2 avoids saturation, as do fixed and rule. It still lowers the RMS battery current by \R{wxb:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\%, but the SC voltage drops to \R{wxb:v2:vmin:mean}\,V, inside the penalised band of \eqref{eq:reward}.

\subsection{Effect of the safety filter}\label{sec:res_filter}
The filter changes the failure behaviour more than the average performance.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{No filter.} One of the three seeds drove the bus out of its admissible range (simulation terminated) on US06, US06$\times1.1$ and WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$. This gives \R{us06a:nof:fails}/9, \R{us06b:nof:fails}/9 and \R{wxb:nof:fails}/9 failed runs. Its saturation on US06 averaged \R{us06a:nof:sat:mean}\,\%.
\item \emph{Filter v1.} No run failed; saturation on US06 was \R{us06a:v1:sat:mean}\,\%.
\item \emph{Filter v2.} No run failed; saturation on US06 was \R{us06a:v2:sat:mean}\,\%. On WLTC extra-high$\times1.2$, saturation dropped from \R{wxb:v1:sat:mean}\,\% (v1) to \R{wxb:v2:sat:mean}\,\%.
\end{itemize}
The filter also narrows the spread between seeds (Table~\ref{tab:test}). On the main test cycles it modified the $v_{sc,ref}$ component of the action in \FiltVscMin--\FiltVscMax\,\% of the decisions, through the rate limit or the bounds, which reflects the fast switching described above.

\subsection{Additional held-out cycles}\label{sec:res_extra}
\input{tab_extra.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:extra} reports three cycles that were introduced after filter v2 was fixed and influenced no design choice.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{WMTC.} v2 lowers the RMS battery current by \R{wmtc:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% (fixed) and \R{wmtc:v2vsarule:dibrmsabs}\,\% (rule) without saturation, whereas v1 saturated \R{wmtc:v1:sat:mean}\,\% of the time.
\item \emph{FTP-MC1b} (mild). The reduction is \R{ftp:v2vsafix:dibrmsabs}\,\% (fixed) and \R{ftp:v2vsarule:dibrmsabs}\,\% (rule).
\item \emph{Long-haul segment} (mostly steady cruising). The agent \emph{increases} the RMS battery current from \R{lh:afix:ibrms}\,A to \R{lh:v2:ibrms:mean}\,A (+\R{lh:v2vsafix:dibrms}\,\%). The same switching of $P_{rec}$ between $\pm\PrecMax$\,kW now has no transient to cancel. It therefore adds a square-wave component of about $\PrecMax\,\mathrm{kW}/\SI{300}{V}\approx\SI{17}{A}$ to an otherwise nearly constant battery current; with the fixed supervisor, the mean $|P_{rec}|$ is \PrecLHfixedAbs\,kW.
\end{itemize}
This is a second, different failure mode outside the training distribution.
